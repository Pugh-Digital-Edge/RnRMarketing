Would evidence that repeat-visitor ads produce additional qualified commercial inquiries change your decision to continue those ads? Before considering a holdout, write down who will make that decision and what result would be useful. Agree on the outcome first: for example, an inquiry from a facility manager with a qualifying loss or service request within your coverage area, confirmed by intake. A page view, form submission, or residential emergency call is not automatically that outcome.

Commercial facilities may plan and consult multiple people before requesting service. Give that decision cycle time to unfold; do not borrow the timing or qualification rules from an emergency residential campaign. The worksheet below is a feasibility check, not a direction to start a study or commit additional spending.

## Describe what the test can answer

Google describes [Conversion Lift](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12003020?hl=en) as a controlled comparison between a treatment group that can see the measured ads and a control group held back from them. The question is whether the measured campaign changes the outcome compared with that holdback, subject to the study design and uncertainty. Last-click reports, view-through reports, and simple before-and-after comparisons cannot isolate that effect on their own.

Record the campaign and audience boundary, the business decision, the outcome definition, and who will review it. Our [retargeting service overview](/services/retargeting/) explains the broader campaign context; this worksheet asks a narrower measurement question.

## Check account and campaign eligibility

First establish whether the proposed study is available for the exact account and campaign. Google's [user-based setup guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12005564?hl=en) says Conversion Lift is not available to every account and directs advertisers to their Google account representative. Its eligible campaign types include Display, Search, Video, Demand Gen, App and Performance Max, with stated exceptions. A campaign already in another lift study has an additional limitation; verify the current rules and your exact configuration with the responsible account owner.

As of November 17, 2026, the same [Google setup guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12005564?hl=en) lists at least 1,000 observed conversions, excluding supplementary conversions from that threshold, and a $5,000 USD minimum campaign budget for this particular user-based study. These are platform-specific prerequisites, not a recommendation to spend, a universal sample size, a guarantee of a useful result, or proof that a specific account qualifies. Ask whether existing eligible volume and budget meet the current requirements; if not, stop the study feasibility review.

## Check the qualified-inquiry event

Google's [setup requirements](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12005564?hl=en) include at least one compatible conversion action. More specifically, ask whether the agreed qualified inquiry can be observed for both the treatment and held-back groups, not merely whether the account tracks some other conversion. Confirm the definition, qualification workflow, data completeness, permitted use, and responsible owner before considering any data-sharing proposal.

The [supported and unsupported setup table](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12005564?hl=en) excludes Google Analytics web conversion imports for this user-based study, even when they contain user-provided data. It also excludes offline imports relying only on click IDs: a person held back from the measured ads may have no ad click to match. The business's responsible party should resolve permission and compatibility questions with the relevant platform representative. This worksheet does not authorize a transfer of customer data or a change to tracking.

A form submission can help diagnose intake volume, but it cannot stand in for a verified commercial inquiry. Google's [guidance on conversion categories](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12005564?hl=en) suggests upper-funnel actions as secondary measures when lower-funnel volume is sparse. Keep such diagnostics clearly separate from the primary decision outcome.

## Estimate the chance of a useful result

Write down existing eligible qualified-inquiry volume, the expected lag from exposure to qualification, the proposed observation period, and the smallest effect that would change the business decision. Also state what uncertainty would leave the decision unresolved. Google's [study power guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12005564?hl=en) uses account-level history, estimated lift and selected settings including conversion actions, duration, budget and holdback split. A high estimated power for a different conversion action does not establish that the qualified-inquiry outcome will be interpretable.

Google's [duration and holdback guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12005564?hl=en) says the period should capture typical conversion lag. It also describes a tradeoff: a larger holdback may increase the measurement sample while forgoing more ad opportunities, whereas a smaller holdback may require longer. Consider that tradeoff with the account owner; neither choice guarantees a conclusive result.

## Document the comparison boundary

List similar campaigns that could reach the same people and the channels outside the study. Google's [setup guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12005564?hl=en) explains that holdback users may still encounter unmeasured campaigns when only a subset is included, making lift harder to detect. Note that limitation before results are reviewed. Do not change those campaigns as part of this worksheet.

## Make the feasibility decision

| Question | Evidence to record | If unresolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exact account and campaign | Platform availability, campaign eligibility and study conflicts | Defer until confirmed. |
| Qualified outcome | Agreed commercial definition and a compatible action observable in both groups | Fix prerequisites; a form alone does not answer the business question. |
| Permission and data | Responsible owner's decision and existing data readiness | Defer; do not transfer customer data on the strength of this worksheet. |
| Volume and resources | Existing observed conversion volume, qualified outcomes, applicable platform thresholds and available budget | Insufficient volume or resources means no-go for this study. |
| Timing and overlap | Qualification lag, planned period and other campaigns that may reach control | Resolve or document the limitation. |
| Decision rule | Minimum useful effect and acceptable uncertainty agreed in advance | Defer until the result could inform a decision. |

Mark **ready for further review** only when every prerequisite is supported and the qualified outcome can plausibly inform the decision. Mark **fix prerequisites** when ownership, permission or measurement is missing. Mark **insufficient volume** when the available observations cannot support an interpretable qualified-outcome comparison, even if some other event is plentiful. These are planning labels, not platform approval.

**Invented no-go example:** A restoration operator records only a handful of qualified commercial inquiries and its offline imports rely solely on click IDs. The qualified outcome cannot be reliably observed in both groups through that setup, and the volume is too low for a useful decision. Defer this specific study. The operator can improve the consistency of its internal CRM qualification record and use existing channel reports descriptively, then recheck current eligibility and volume with the responsible parties. Descriptive totals do not establish incremental leads.

## Read a final result against the decision rule

Google's [reporting guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14102450?hl=en) recommends waiting until the study ends for the most accurate lift result, even though cumulative results may appear during it. Allow for the agreed qualification lag and examine the qualified outcome, the reported interval, counts and study limitations together.

For illustration only, imagine a completed study with few observed qualified conversions and an estimated relative lift of 10%, with an interval from -40% to +60%. The interval crosses zero and spans effects that could lead to different decisions. This hypothetical result is too uncertain for the stated decision; it proves neither success nor failure. Google's [interpretation guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14102450?hl=en) warns that low control conversion counts can produce unusually large relative-lift estimates and provides confidence intervals to help interpret results. Record the uncertainty and decide whether the evidence merits further review, rather than converting the point estimate into a lead guarantee.
