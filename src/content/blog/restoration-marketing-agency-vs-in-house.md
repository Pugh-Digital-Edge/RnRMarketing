**Choose a restoration marketing staffing model by mapping the work, decisions, coverage, and total cost your company actually needs.** An in-house manager can own recurring marketing operations, while an agency can provide a defined set of external capabilities. A hybrid can place a decision-maker inside the business while specialists execute selected work. Every model still needs operational input and a named approver.

Start with the next 90 days, not a job title or a sales pitch. List the services, branches, territories, channels, and deliverables in scope. Then compare each option against that same assignment and the time your team can actually give it.

## Inventory the Work Before Choosing a Model

Use a normal week and a busy week. Enter actual volume and hours where known; mark unknowns for a short time log rather than inventing them. For each row, name the person accountable for the result, the person doing the work, an approver, and a backup. One person may fill several roles.

| Campaign planning and execution | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ |
| Website, landing-page and tracking changes | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ |
| Creative, content and factual review | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ |
| Intake feedback and lead disposition | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ |
| Reporting, analysis and decisions | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ |
| After-hours exceptions and absence coverage | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ |

Record when each handoff is needed. For example, who tells marketing that a branch cannot serve a territory, who approves the change, and who updates a campaign? Identify who handles intake and urgent exceptions outside normal hours; an agency engagement does not automatically include either. Check the proposed response window against your real operating schedule.

The [Bureau of Labor Statistics' marketing manager profile](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/advertising-promotions-and-marketing-managers.htm) describes work that includes discussing budgets and contracts, planning campaigns, researching markets, and directing staff. Use those functions to test a candidate's scope: a manager who coordinates specialists may not also have capacity to execute every technical task.

## Match Capabilities and Backup to the Work

Separate ongoing coordination from occasional specialist work. A person may have several skills, and a part-time specialist or bounded project may cover a gap. Ask each candidate or provider for examples relevant to the assignment, the named executor, an escalation path, and coverage during leave or turnover.

BLS describes [market research analysts](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/market-research-analysts.htm) as measuring marketing effectiveness and presenting findings; its May 2025 national median annual wage for that occupation was $78,760. For your worksheet, identify who can interpret the data, explain uncertainty, and recommend a decision—not just export a dashboard.

Website development is another capability to test. BLS reports a May 2025 national median annual wage of $92,650 for [web developers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/web-developers.htm). Define who can make and check the specific site and tracking changes you need, and who can take over if that person is unavailable.

For visual work, BLS reports a May 2025 national median annual wage of $62,960 for [graphic designers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/arts-and-design/graphic-designers.htm), whose duties include developing layouts, revising designs, and reviewing work for errors. Identify who prepares assets and who checks that service and territory claims are accurate before use.

BLS reports a May 2025 national median annual wage of $166,790 for [marketing managers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/advertising-promotions-and-marketing-managers.htm). All four figures above are national occupational wage context across employers, not restoration-specific salaries, local offers, total employment costs, or a requirement to hire four people. Do not add the medians together as the inevitable cost of an in-house option. Scope and actual candidates determine the work and pay to compare.

For wage context, BLS estimated June 2026 [private-industry compensation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.htm) across occupations at $46.89 per hour worked: $32.82 in wages and $14.07 in benefits. Its separate estimate for [private-industry establishments with 1–49 workers](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.htm) was $37.64 per hour worked, including $9.76 in benefits. Neither aggregate is a restoration or manager-specific estimate, an offer, or a multiplier to apply mechanically to your candidate's wage.

BLS groups the benefits in its [compensation estimates](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.htm) into paid leave, supplemental pay, insurance, retirement and savings, and legally required benefits. Use actual applicable costs in your worksheet and avoid counting the same employer-paid item twice, including legally required benefits already included in an aggregate.

The IRS explains that employers generally withhold employees' Social Security and Medicare taxes and pay an employer share; federal unemployment tax is reported and paid separately. Review its [employment-tax guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/understanding-employment-taxes) with your adviser. Gross employee pay already includes employee withholding: do not add that withholding again as an employer expense. Estimate applicable employer-paid costs separately.

The SBA's [payroll planning guide](https://legacy.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business/hire-manage-employees) includes planning leave, choosing payroll administration, and identifying records and reporting. Put the time and cost of those responsibilities, recruiting, and backup coverage into the employee option where applicable.

Calling a person a contractor does not settle their classification. For federal employment tax purposes, the IRS considers behavioral control, financial control, and the relationship between the parties in its [worker-classification guidance](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc762). Ask a qualified adviser to assess the actual arrangement and applicable rules before relying on a classification.

## Decide Who Owns Each Handoff

| Responsibility | In-house-led | Agency-led | Hybrid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Business priorities, territory and branch knowledge | Company owner or operations lead supplies and approves | Company supplies and approves | Company supplies; internal manager coordinates |
| Campaign execution | Employee or named specialist | Named agency executor within scope | Agency or specialist executes; manager coordinates |
| Intake and lead disposition | Company intake owner | Company intake owner; agency receives agreed feedback | Company intake owner; manager reconciles feedback |
| Reporting and recommendations | Employee or specialist | Agency prepares within scope | Agency and manager divide preparation and interpretation |
| Spend, claims and material changes | Named company approver | Named company approver | Named company approver, with manager coordinating |
| Absence or after-hours issue | Named backup and escalation | Named company and agency contacts, within agreed coverage | Named backups on both sides |

These are starting assignments, not fixed rules. Put names, decision limits, response windows, and escalation contacts into the agreement or job plan. If no one can approve work promptly, the model needs more internal capacity regardless of who executes it.

For continuity, document access and transition responsibilities. Google says an administrator can grant, change, or remove users' access to an individual account in its [Google Ads access guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6372672?hl=en_us_us). Assign an appropriate company administrator and review access when roles change, consistent with your security practices and agreements.

## Choose a Model and Review It at 90 Days

An **in-house manager** may fit when recurring coordination and decisions fill a meaningful role, the owner can recruit and manage that person, and specialist gaps and absence coverage have owners. An **agency** may fit when the defined work calls for external skills or variable capacity and the company can provide timely operational direction and approvals. A **hybrid** may fit when an internal manager can translate branch priorities and oversee external specialists without duplicating their work. A manager can coordinate an agency; the roles are not mutually exclusive.

For a hypothetical company with an owner who has limited approval time and an occasional website development need, the worksheet might reveal that neither a broad agency scope nor a full-time developer alone solves the bottleneck. It could test a named internal decision-maker plus a bounded external project, with a backup approver. The example is a way to use the worksheet, not a forecast or a recommended budget.

Before starting, agree on the deliverables, accountable people, decision deadlines, cost assumptions, and a review date. At 30 days check whether access, branch information, and handoffs are working. At 60 days review completed work, unresolved dependencies, and coverage. At 90 days compare actual workload and costs with the agreed scope, review available business evidence, and decide whether to continue, change responsibilities, or seek another capability. No staffing model guarantees a result.
