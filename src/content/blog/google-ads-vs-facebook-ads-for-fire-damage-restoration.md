**Choose the next channel by the decision you need a prospect to make and the response your team can actually deliver.** For a restoration company, a search for urgent fire damage help and a planned conversation with a commercial property manager are different situations. Google Search and Facebook advertising can support different parts of that work; neither platform guarantees a qualified lead or a lower cost.

This worksheet helps an owner decide what to test, when to wait, and what evidence would change the decision. It complements the [fire damage marketing guide](/resources/fire-damage-restoration-marketing/), which covers the broader marketing system, and the [Google Ads framework](/resources/google-ads-for-restoration-companies/), which explains campaign execution.

## Start with the decision, not the platform

| Owner question | Google Search: consider first when… | Facebook ads: consider first when… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What situation are we addressing? | You want to answer an active search for a fire restoration service you can provide. | You want to introduce an appropriate service or educational resource to an audience before a specific need is known. |
| What should the person do next? | Call or request an inspection through a clear service-specific path. | Read a relevant resource, request information, call, or start a conversation appropriate to the offer. |
| Who will respond? | Someone can handle urgent inquiries during the hours the campaign runs. | Someone owns timely follow-up for the form, call, or message experience selected. |
| What must the creative show? | Accurate service and territory information that fits the search and landing page. | A clear, respectful offer without implying that the advertiser knows a person recently experienced a fire. |
| What would pause the test? | No reliable call coverage, unavailable capacity, or an irrelevant landing path. | No clear audience/offer, no follow-up owner, or no dependable way to record responses. |

These are planning choices, not universal platform rules. Do not assume an audience setting identifies a household currently experiencing a fire. For a commercial property manager, a useful next action may be a planned conversation about preparedness; for someone actively seeking help, a form left without timely follow-up can be the wrong handoff. Use the company's actual coverage and capacity to decide.

## What each channel can tell you

Google Search offers a way to match advertising to a person's search. **Google Ads keyword match types govern how closely a search must relate to a keyword for the ad to be considered.** Even exact match can include searches with the same meaning or intent, rather than only identical wording. The [Google Ads matching guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529?hl=en&ref_type=adv) explains the distinctions. That makes observed search language useful for an urgent-service test, while the actual inquiries still need review.

Google allows negative keywords to exclude ads from searches containing specified terms, but negative match rules differ from positive match rules. The [matching guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529?hl=en&ref_type=adv) is a starting point for reviewing irrelevant queries. The [search terms report](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472708?hl=en_us_us) lists sufficiently active searches that showed an ad, and those searches can differ from the keyword list. It is not a complete census of every search, so an empty or partial report does not prove all traffic was relevant.

For Meta, the response path is a choice to make before launch. Its [lead-generation training](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/activity/393363-generate-leads-across-meta-technologies?sid=67e90b3a-4170-4306-8191-69e4eefacb65&sid_i=22) describes instant forms, website forms, calls and messaging as available lead-ad experiences. Select one your team can handle: a form can be appropriate for a planned discussion; a call path needs coverage while it is offered. These are examples, not guarantees that an individual will use either channel as intended.

## Work through the allocation worksheet

Put the two options side by side on a sheet before assigning a budget. For each row, write a Search answer and a Facebook answer, then mark each **ready**, **needs work**, or **wait**. A channel is not ready just because an ad can be created:

1. **Opportunity:** Which fire-related service and territory can we handle now? Is the desired prospect seeking immediate help or considering a future commercial relationship?
2. **Offer and next action:** What useful, accurate promise can the ad make? Where will a person land, and what can they do next without guessing?
3. **Response capacity:** Who answers calls or monitors messages and forms, including after hours? If nobody owns the handoff, fix that before buying more responses.
4. **Creative and fit:** Does the message explain the service without exploiting a distressing event or claiming to know the viewer's circumstances? Can the team serve the inquiry it invites?
5. **Evidence:** What constitutes a qualified opportunity, and where will the team record the next step, duplicate, out-of-area request, or unknown source?
6. **Test boundary:** Set a budget and review date the business can afford; record what will cause a pause, adjustment, or continuation before the test begins.

For example, a company with crews available in its service area and staffed intake might mark the urgent Search path ready, while marking Facebook wait until a commercial preparedness offer and follow-up owner are defined. If intake is unavailable, the same company should mark the urgent path wait even if its landing page is complete. These are readiness examples, not a prediction about lead volume.

If Search has a clear service-specific response path but the social offer is still vague, begin by improving the Search path. If a commercial education offer has a defined audience and follow-up owner but urgent calls cannot be answered, resolve call coverage before treating paid search as an emergency solution. If neither path is ready, delay both tests. These are conditional decisions, not a forecast of results.

## Compare outcomes with the same definitions

Record each unique inquiry once, then distinguish an eligible conversation, a booked next step, and authorized work. Agree on the service area and time window before comparing channels. The [lead-tracking service overview](/services/lead-tracking/) and [restoration marketing ROI guide](/resources/restoration-marketing-roi/) explain the broader reconciliation process; this comparison worksheet should not turn two platform dashboards into two counts of the same job.

A Google Ads call conversion can depend on an advertiser-defined call-duration threshold; total calls can exceed conversions. A long call is not proof that the inquiry was qualified. Google also reports calls on the date they occur but call conversions on the date of the ad click, so dates can differ in a comparison. Check the [call-reporting definitions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7180997?hl=en) before interpreting a difference between dashboards.

Some opportunities become jobs after the online interaction. [Google's offline conversion overview](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2998031?hl=en) explains that imported offline conversions can measure later events after an ad click or call. Meta's [CRM training](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/activity/603002-improve-lead-quality-with-meta-conversions-api-for-crm?sid=0c6fde23-8d2a-4fc7-b9aa-3e3052378010&sid_i=1) describes lead-generation campaigns using instant or website forms and its Conversions API for CRM to optimize for qualified leads; the same training advises monitoring integration status and lead coverage. These options require appropriate setup and data practices, and neither makes a recorded event proof of revenue or a qualified job on its own.

Attribution models assign credit to touchpoints on a path to an important action, as [Google Analytics explains](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/16291112?hl=en). That credit is a reporting method, not a causal experiment or a deduplicated count of jobs. Keep unknown sources visible, and use actual job records where available.

## Decide what to do after the test

At the review date, look first for a broken handoff: unanswered calls, unworked forms, wrong territories, or missing source and disposition. If those prevent a fair comparison, repair the process and document the limitation before reallocating spend. If the records are usable, compare the same defined outcome and period, alongside the cost included for each channel. Small numbers and long sales cycles can make a winner unclear; extend or redesign the test when the business has enough capacity and budget to do so.

The strongest allocation is the one supported by your own service fit, response capacity, and verified outcomes.
